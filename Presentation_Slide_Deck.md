## Slide 3: STATISTICAL HYPOTHESIS TESTING: WHAT THE DATA TELLS US
*Rigorous Evaluation: Do Night Shifts and Low Hikes Actually Drive Exits?* | **Suggested Time: 1:15 min**

### Slide Key Points:
- Hypothesis 1 (Night Shifts): STRONGLY SUPPORTED. Night shift attrition is 57.3% vs 41.7% Day (OR = 1.88x, p < 0.0001).
- Hypothesis 2 (Base Pay): REFUTED. Base salaries differ by < ₹300 between leavers and stayers (Cohen's d = 0.032, p = 0.42).
- The Real Compensation Driver: APPRAISAL HIKE COMPRESSION. Sub-6% hikes increase exit risk by 2.34x (p < 0.0001).
- Compounding Hazard: Night Shift + Low Hike (<6%) results in an alarming 68.4% attrition rate.

### Speaker Notes:
> We rigorously tested management's assumptions using Welch's t-tests and Chi-Square tests of independence. The verdict? Team leaders were right about night shifts—night shift agents are 1.88 times more likely to quit. But they were mistaken about base pay: base salaries are virtually identical. The real financial trigger is appraisal stagnation: receiving a sub-6% hike increases turnover risk by 2.34 times, and combining night shifts with low hikes spikes attrition to 68.4%.
